# ExamTestAI, launch creative: the scroll-stopper

19 August 2026. For ChatGPT Image 2.0. examtestai.com

**Brand mark on every image: ExamTestAI, bottom right, small, low opacity.**

---

## What the research changed

**1. Facebook, not Instagram.**
Facebook has 17.63 million users in Nepal, 54.6% of the whole population, and
the largest group is 25 to 34. Students are 45.4% of all social media users.
Instagram is 5.35 million and skews 18 to 24. So the master's applicant, who
is your paying customer, is on Facebook. Build for the Facebook feed first.

**2. This market runs on fear, not aspiration.**
Nepal is on the list of countries where the pre-CAS interview is effectively
mandatory. Refusals rose in early 2026. The published failure reasons are
specific: not knowing your own course or university, poor spoken English,
unclear career plans, and answers that contradict your own documents.

Every other consultancy ad sells the destination. Nobody is selling the thirty
minutes that decides it.

**3. Trust beats hype here, and hype actively costs you.**
Nepali service buying is driven by trust and social proof. Exaggerated claims
reduce trust. Every competitor in that feed is shouting "100% visa success".
**Matching them makes you invisible. Undercutting them makes you credible.**

---

## The scroll-stopping idea

Scroll a Nepali student's Facebook feed and you see the same picture forty
times: Big Ben, a graduation gown, an aeroplane window, a flag, a smiling group,
bright saturated blue and gold, "100% SUCCESS".

**The pattern break is not a better version of that picture. It is the picture
nobody is running.**

Everyone shows the destination. **Show the moment before.**

A Nepali student, alone, in their own room, in the ninety seconds before the
interview call connects. That image is not in the feed. It is instantly
recognisable, because it is their room. And it is the exact moment they are
afraid of.

**Then the oven promise lands on top of it: you do not have to walk into that
moment guessing. Ten questions, free, and you will know.**

---

## Art direction

**Dark, quiet, one accent colour.** This is the whole trick. In a feed of bright
saturated ads, a dark restrained image reads as different and as serious. It
also matches the product, which is the honest one.

| | |
|---|---|
| Base | Deep ink navy, near black |
| Light | One warm source, a laptop screen or a window |
| Accent | A single signal green, used once and only once |
| Never | Big Ben, gowns, aeroplanes, flags, stock handshakes, gold gradients |
| Never | A phone or laptop showing the app interface |
| Type | One heavy sans headline. Nothing decorative |

## Concept 1: The ninety seconds before

**The lead concept. Run this one first.**

> A cinematic photograph of a young Nepali man in his early twenties sitting
> alone at a small desk in a modest Kathmandu bedroom at night. He is wearing a
> plain formal shirt over an ordinary vest, dressed properly only from the
> chest up. He sits very upright, hands flat on the desk, waiting. His face is
> lit only by the cool glow of an unseen laptop screen just below the frame,
> which is not visible to us. His expression is tense concentration, not fear,
> not a smile. Behind him the room is real and lived in: a painted wall with a
> visible crack, a shelf of folders, clothes on a hook, a window with thin
> curtains showing dark blue night. The room is dim, deliberately underexposed,
> deep navy shadows, one warm bulb far in the background out of focus. Shot on
> a 50mm lens at f/2, shallow depth of field, natural imperfect lighting,
> documentary photography, not a stock photo, no logos, no text, no visible
> screens. Muted colour grade, desaturated, cinematic.

**Headline over the image:** In thirty minutes, they decide.
**Beneath it:** Sit it once for free, before it counts.
**Corner chip, the single green element:** 10 questions free
**Brand, bottom right:** ExamTestAI

## Concept 2: The empty chair

**The strongest one for a cold audience, because it has no face to dismiss.**

> A cinematic photograph of an empty wooden chair pulled up to a small desk in a
> dim Nepali bedroom, photographed straight on from where an interviewer would
> be. The desk is bare except for a closed notebook and a glass of water. The
> chair is turned very slightly, as though someone has just stood up, or has not
> yet sat down. The only light is a cool blue-white glow falling on the empty
> seat from an unseen screen. Everything else falls away into deep navy
> darkness. Real, lived-in room, a little worn, honest. Shot on a 35mm lens,
> f/2.8, shallow focus on the chair, heavy shadow, cinematic colour grade,
> desaturated, documentary style, no people, no text, no logos, no visible
> screens.

**Headline:** This is the chair that decides it.
**Beneath:** Practise in it ten times before anyone is watching.
**Green chip:** 10 questions free
**Brand, bottom right:** ExamTestAI

## Concept 3: The oven, literally

**Your own metaphor, and it works because it is about certainty, not food.**

The point of an oven is not heat. It is that **you stop guessing.** The timer
runs, the ding comes, and you know. Right now a student has no timer and no
ding. They walk in hoping.

> A tight cinematic photograph of a single old kitchen timer sitting on a bare
> dark wooden desk beside a closed passport and a folded shirt collar. The timer
> is a plain mechanical dial, scuffed and real, not new. It is lit from one side
> by warm lamplight, with the rest of the frame falling into deep navy shadow.
> Extremely shallow depth of field on the timer dial. Quiet, still, serious.
> Shot on an 85mm macro lens at f/2, cinematic colour grade, desaturated,
> product-documentary style, no text, no logos, no branding on any object.

**Headline:** Every other student walks in guessing.
**Beneath:** Ten questions, marked honestly, so you know before you go.
**Green chip:** Free
**Brand, bottom right:** ExamTestAI

## Concept 4: The two envelopes

**Use only as the second or third ad in the sequence, never as the first.**
It names the fear directly, which works on an audience that already knows you.

> A cinematic overhead photograph of two identical plain white envelopes lying
> side by side on a dark worn wooden table, lit by one hard shaft of window
> light from the left. The envelope on the left lies flat and unopened. The
> envelope on the right has been torn open roughly and is empty, its torn flap
> curling. Deep shadow across the right of the frame. Real paper texture, dust
> in the light, no text on the envelopes, no logos, no stamps. Shot from
> directly above on a 50mm lens, f/4, cinematic desaturated colour grade,
> documentary still life.

**Headline:** One of these is a refusal.
**Beneath:** The interview is where it is decided. Sit it free, first.
**Green chip:** 10 questions free
**Brand, bottom right:** ExamTestAI

## The post copy

Nepali-English mix, short lines, no exclamation marks. Trust, not shouting.

**Version A, the direct one**

> Most Nepali students applying to the UK now get a pre-CAS interview.
>
> Most of them have never done one.
>
> You get 10 questions free. Real interview, real timer, camera on. At the end
> you are told, honestly, where you actually stand. Not a certificate. Not a
> guaranteed pass. The truth, while you can still do something about it.
>
> Sign in with Google. That is the whole setup.

**Version B, the one that names the competitor without naming them**

> Some practice tools will give you a score even if you say nothing.
>
> We will not. If we cannot hear you, we tell you. If your answer was weak, we
> tell you that too, and we show you how to say it better.
>
> 10 questions free. No payment, no consultancy, no appointment.

## What to test first

Run **Concept 1 and Concept 2 against each other**, same copy, Facebook feed,
Nepal, age 20 to 30, interest in study abroad and UK universities.

Judge on one number: **how many finish the tenth question**, not clicks. A click
costs you nothing. A finished trial is a student who has seen the product work.

---

## One honest caution

The free trial is your best hook and your biggest risk at the same time. Every
free sitting costs you real money at a provider, and marketing brings volume
before it brings buyers.

**Watch the `/super` dashboard daily for the first week**, specifically "Paid
calls this month" and the "accounts on this number" badge. If the same WhatsApp
number appears on several accounts, that is not a marketing win, it is one
person spending your budget.
